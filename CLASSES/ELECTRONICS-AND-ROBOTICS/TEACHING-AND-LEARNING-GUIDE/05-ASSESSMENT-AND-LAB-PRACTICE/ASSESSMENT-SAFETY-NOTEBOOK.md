# Assessment, Lab Safety, and Engineering Notebooks

## 1. Assess practical understanding

Assessment should measure whether students can build, understand, test, and improve a system—not only recall definitions.

### Suggested project rubric

| Assessment area | Weight |
|---|---:|
| Circuit construction and correct connections | 20% |
| Understanding of electronics concepts | 20% |
| Project functionality | 20% |
| Testing and troubleshooting | 20% |
| Explanation, documentation, and teamwork | 20% |
| **Total** | **100%** |

Adapt the rubric to the project and age group. Do not penalize a student simply because a prototype has a fault if the student can diagnose it, explain it, and show evidence of improvement.

## 2. Engineering Notebook

For each project, students should maintain a concise record containing:

- Project objective
- Circuit diagram or build sketch
- Components used
- Initial prediction
- Build notes or photographs
- Test conditions and results
- Problems encountered
- Changes made
- Final explanation
- Limitations or next improvement

Keep the notebook practical and brief. It should record the engineering process rather than become a long theory assignment.

## 3. Lab safety

Teach safety before students begin building, then reinforce it during each project.

- Use only approved low-voltage power sources for student circuits.
- Never connect student breadboard circuits directly to household mains electricity.
- Check polarity before connecting batteries, LEDs, and other polarized components.
- Avoid short-circuiting a battery or power supply.
- Disconnect power before changing wiring.
- Use motors, pumps, and other higher-current devices with suitable drivers and power sources.
- Teach multimeter use before asking students to measure circuits.
- Keep water away from powered circuits. Water-based projects require appropriate separation and supervision.
- Ask students to check the circuit before switching it on.
- Follow the school's lab rules and teacher supervision requirements.

For relays or other switching devices, use an appropriately rated, teacher-prepared low-voltage demonstration. Do not have students handle mains wiring.

## 4. Component kit preparation

Prepare a project kit for each student group. Each kit should include:
- Project component list
- Quantity of each item
- Required tools
- Power-source specification
- Circuit diagram or build reference
- Safety notes
- Check-in/check-out list

Use the school's confirmed ATL inventory. Do not assume a component is available just because it appears in a generic project guide.

## 5. Group work

For team projects, rotate roles such as:
- Builder
- Circuit checker
- Test recorder
- Presenter

Every student should get a chance to connect, test, explain, and troubleshoot. Teamwork should not mean that one student does all the electronics.

## 6. Evidence of learning

Useful evidence includes:
- A correctly assembled circuit
- A student-drawn circuit diagram
- Sensor readings or test results
- A clear explanation of input, processing, and output
- A documented fault and how it was diagnosed
- A demonstrated improvement
- A reflection on limitations
