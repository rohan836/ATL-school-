# Class 5 — Electronics and Robotics Curriculum

**Course focus:** Discover electricity and build simple circuits using safe, low-voltage components.

## Learning sequence

| Unit | Concept | Practical activity or build |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Electronics in everyday life | Identify electronic devices and discuss what they do |
| 2 | Lab safety and low voltage | Recognize safe lab practices and approved power sources |
| 3 | Conductors and insulators | Test materials using a simple circuit |
| 4 | Open and closed circuits | Build and break a circuit using a switch |
| 5 | Power source, wires, and LED | Light an LED in a complete circuit |
| 6 | LED polarity | Reverse the LED and observe; learn correct orientation |
| 7 | Resistors | Compare LED brightness using suitable resistor values |
| 8 | Switches | Build a switch-controlled light |
| 9 | Series circuits | Connect two LEDs in series and observe |
| 10 | Parallel circuits | Build and compare a parallel LED circuit |
| 11 | Buzzer | Build a simple sound circuit |
| 12 | Simple alarm | Make a switch-triggered door or lid alarm |
| 13 | Light and distance sensing | Explore an LDR or distance sensor with teacher guidance |
| 14 | Inputs and outputs | Identify what detects and what responds |
| 15 | Project build | Create a distance alarm or automatic light |
| 16 | Test and present | Test in different conditions, explain, and improve |

## Capstone

**Distance alarm or automatic light.** Students should identify the power source, circuit path, input (if used), output, and how the device responds.

## Expected outcomes

Students can build a basic circuit, use an LED and resistor correctly, explain open/closed circuits, identify conductors and insulators, and describe how a simple sensor-based device responds.

## Practical note

Keep activities at safe low voltage. Select the final project according to actual component availability and student readiness.
