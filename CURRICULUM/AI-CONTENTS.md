**1. What AI is** — machines that learn patterns from data, not follow fixed rules.
**2. Data, pattern, prediction** — the three words behind every AI system.
**3. Features and labels** — what the machine looks at, and what it is told the answer is.
**4. Classification and generation** — sorting things into groups vs producing new things.
**5. Building a dataset** — categories, examples, variety.
**6. Training a model** — teaching the machine from examples.
**7. Testing and accuracy** — checking it on things it has not seen.
**8. Data quality and bias** — why bad examples give bad answers.
**9. Train and test data** — why a model that memorises looks smart and isn't.
**10. The model card** — recording what it can do and where it fails.
**11. Sensors** — light, distance, temperature as machine senses.
**12. Decision rules** — thresholds and if-this-then-that.
**13. Actuators** — servos, relays, LEDs: making a decision act.
**14. Model and hardware together** — a classifier driving something real.
**15. Capstone project** — build a working device.
**16. Test, document, demonstrate** — prove it works and state its limits.

**17. Python and data** — reading a dataset on the Raspberry Pi.
**18. Train/test split and confusion matrix** — measuring properly.

## Who covers what

- Classes 5–6: units 1–16, slower pace.
- Classes 7–8: units 1–16, more measurement.
- Class 9: units 1–20.

## Rules that apply to every unit

- No face recognition, no photographs of students, no student data uploaded anywhere.
- Gas sensors are teaching components, not life-safety alarms.
- Never drive a servo, motor, pump, or relay coil directly from a GPIO pin.
- An idea is not a result: every claim needs a measured accuracy or a recorded test.
- Builds powered from mains are out of scope.
