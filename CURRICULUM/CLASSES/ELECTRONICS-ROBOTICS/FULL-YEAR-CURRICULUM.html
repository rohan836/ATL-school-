Electronics & Robotics Curriculum

One shared eight-module curriculum for Classes 5–9

ModuleTopics and practical work
Module 1: Discovering ElectricityWhat is electricity?; atoms, electrons, and electric charge; conductors and insulators; open and closed circuits; batteries, wires, switches, and loads. Practical: Build a simple LED circuit.
Module 2: Voltage, Current & ResistanceVoltage: electrical push; current: flow of charge; resistance: opposition to current; AC and DC; series and parallel circuits; Ohm's Law: V = I × R; resistor color codes. Practical: Measure voltage, current, and resistance.
Module 3: Electronic ComponentsResistors; LEDs and diodes; capacitors; potentiometers; LDRs and thermistors; push buttons and switches. Practical: Component identification and testing.
Module 4: Transistors & SwitchingWhat is a transistor?; NPN and PNP; base, collector, emitter; transistor as switch and amplifier; relay working principle. Projects: LED switch, touch switch, relay control.
Module 5: Sensors & Output DevicesWhat is a sensor?; LDR, IR, ultrasonic, and temperature sensors; buzzers and speakers; DC motors; servo motors; motor drivers. Projects: Night lamp, alarm, distance indicator.
Module 6: Introduction to MicrocontrollersMicrocontrollers; Arduino / ESP32; digital input/output; analog input; PWM and brightness control; reading sensors; programming basics. Projects: Traffic light, sensor alarm, servo control.
Module 7: Robotics FoundationsWhat is a robot?; inputs, processing, outputs; wheels, chassis, mechanisms; DC motor control; servo mechanisms; obstacle detection. Projects: Mini robot, obstacle-avoiding robot.
Module 8: Design & InnovationIdentify a real-world problem; plan a circuit; select components; build a prototype; test and troubleshoot; improve the design. Final project: Exhibition.

Lab Safety

  • Student-built circuits use low-voltage DC. No probing household mains or building exposed mains-powered circuits.
  • Teach AC with diagrams or teacher-prepared isolated low-voltage demonstrations.
  • Use current-limiting resistors and suitable motor drivers/power supplies. Never drive motors or high-current servos directly from GPIO pins.
  • Check board pinouts, logic levels, and component ratings; supervise tools and soldering.
