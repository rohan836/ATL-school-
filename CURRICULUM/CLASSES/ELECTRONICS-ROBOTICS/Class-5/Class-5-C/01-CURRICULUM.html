Course focus: Discover electricity and build simple circuits using safe, low-voltage components.

Learning sequence

UnitConceptPractical activity or build
1Electronics in everyday lifeIdentify electronic devices and discuss what they do
2Lab safety and low voltageRecognize safe lab practices and approved power sources
3Conductors and insulatorsTest materials using a simple circuit
4Open and closed circuitsBuild and break a circuit using a switch
5Power source, wires, and LEDLight an LED in a complete circuit
6LED polarityReverse the LED and observe; learn correct orientation
7ResistorsCompare LED brightness using suitable resistor values
8SwitchesBuild a switch-controlled light
9Series circuitsConnect two LEDs in series and observe
10Parallel circuitsBuild and compare a parallel LED circuit
11BuzzerBuild a simple sound circuit
12Simple alarmMake a switch-triggered door or lid alarm
13Light and distance sensingExplore an LDR or distance sensor with teacher guidance
14Inputs and outputsIdentify what detects and what responds
15Project buildCreate a distance alarm or automatic light
16Test and presentTest in different conditions, explain, and improve

Capstone

Distance alarm or automatic light. Students should identify the power source, circuit path, input (if used), output, and how the device responds.

Expected outcomes

Students can build a basic circuit, use an LED and resistor correctly, explain open/closed circuits, identify conductors and insulators, and describe how a simple sensor-based device responds.

Practical note

Keep activities at safe low voltage. Select the final project according to actual component availability and student readiness.
