# Electronics & Robotics Curriculum — Classes 5–9

**Atal Thinking Lab | Progressive, hands-on learning**

| Class | Curriculum sequence | Practical experiments and projects |
|---|---|---|
| Class 5 | Electricity basics; electrical charge; conductors and insulators; cells and batteries; open and closed circuits; switches; simple DC circuits; LED polarity | Build a battery-switch-bulb circuit; test conductors and insulators; identify LED polarity; make a simple LED circuit |
| Class 6 | Voltage, current, and resistance; AC and DC concepts; series and parallel circuits; multimeter basics; resistor identification and color code; Ohm’s law (V = I × R); LED current limiting | Measure battery voltage; measure resistor values; test continuity; calculate and measure LED current; compare series and parallel circuits; build a potentiometer-controlled LED |
| Class 7 | Electronic components: resistors, diodes, LEDs, capacitors, potentiometers, LDRs, thermistors, switches; transistor basics; NPN/PNP; base, collector, emitter; transistor as a switch; relay working principle | Identify and test components; observe capacitor charging/discharging; build a transistor LED switch; make an LDR night lamp; operate a buzzer with a transistor; control a low-voltage load using a relay module |
| Class 8 | Sensors and outputs; analog and digital signals; DC motors; servo motors; motor drivers; microcontroller basics with Arduino/ESP32; digital input/output; analog input; PWM; introductory programming | Read an LDR or temperature sensor; make a sensor alarm; control LED brightness with PWM; rotate a servo using a potentiometer; control a DC motor through a motor driver; build a sensor-triggered mechanism |
| Class 9 | ESP32/microcontroller applications; sensor integration; motor and servo control; robot structure and mechanisms; input–processing–output systems; obstacle detection; automation; design, testing, debugging, and iteration | Build an ESP32-based monitoring or control project; make an obstacle-detecting robot; develop an automated mechanism; integrate sensors, motors, and a servo into a working prototype; complete a student-designed robotics project |

## Lab Safety

- Student-built circuits use low-voltage DC. Students must not probe household mains or build exposed mains-powered circuits.
- Teach AC concepts with diagrams or teacher-prepared, isolated low-voltage demonstrations. Keep relay projects on the low-voltage side.
- Use current-limiting resistors and check component polarity before powering a circuit.
- Do not drive motors or high-current servos directly from microcontroller GPIO pins. Use suitable drivers and power supplies.
- Verify board/module pinouts, logic levels, and current/voltage limits; supervise tools and soldering.
