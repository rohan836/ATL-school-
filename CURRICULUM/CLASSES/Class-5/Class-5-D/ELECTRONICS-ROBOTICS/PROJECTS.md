# Class 5 — Interactive Electronics Projects

Short, playful builds reinforce the curriculum. Use teacher-approved low-voltage power only. Every LED circuit must include a suitable current-limiting resistor.

## 1. Circuit Detective
**Curriculum:** conductors/insulators, open/closed circuits, LED polarity, resistor.
**Challenge:** Make an LED light, then test which mystery material completes the circuit.
**Materials:** low-voltage battery pack, LED, suitable resistor, wires, switch/paper clip, teacher-selected materials.
1. Build a working LED circuit with a switch.
2. Open the circuit and predict the result.
3. Test materials one at a time in the gap (metal, foil, paper, plastic, wood).
4. Record predictions and observations; swap mystery materials with another team.
**Interactive twist:** score correct predictions, not just successful circuits.
**Success:** explain the current path and why a break stops the circuit.

## 2. Reaction-Time Light Game
**Curriculum:** switches, buzzer, inputs and outputs.
**Challenge:** Make a signal and play a reaction game.
**Materials:** low-voltage circuit parts, LED/buzzer, switch or button, cardboard target.
1. Build a button-controlled LED or buzzer.
2. A caller gives an unpredictable GO cue; players race to touch a target or press a safe button.
3. Change one game rule and discuss fair testing.
**Interactive twist:** teams invent and exchange game rules. This is not electronic timing unless a suitable timer is available.
**Success:** identify input, output, and switch behavior.

## 3. Mini Door / Box Alarm
**Curriculum:** switches and simple alarms.
**Challenge:** Alert the team when a cardboard door or box opens.
**Materials:** low-voltage pack, buzzer, switch/contact, wires, cardboard.
1. Position the switch so opening the flap changes circuit state.
2. Test open and closed states.
3. Adjust placement for reliable triggering.
4. Ask another team to open it and observe the alarm.
**Success:** demonstrate both states and explain how the switch detects position.

## 4. Sensor Surprise: Night Light or Distance Alert
**Curriculum:** LDR/distance sensing, inputs/outputs, testing.
**Challenge:** Make an output respond to darkness or a nearby object.
**Materials:** available LDR or distance sensor and its approved circuit/controller setup.
1. Observe sensor behavior under two conditions.
2. Choose a simple trigger with teacher help.
3. Build a night light or distance alert.
4. Test different light levels/distances and record activation.
**Success:** describe what is sensed and how the device responds. If parts are unavailable, use the box alarm as the capstone.

## Safety
- Never connect an LED directly to a battery without a suitable resistor.
- No mains electricity or unknown power supplies.
- Keep circuits dry; do not test unknown liquids.
- Sensor projects depend on actual inventory.
